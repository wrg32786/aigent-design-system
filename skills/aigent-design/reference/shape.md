# Shape

Use before visual direction when the product, task, content, states, or scope is materially unclear. Return a confirmed brief and stop before code.

## Ask only what changes the work

One round of two or three related questions is usually enough:

- Who arrives, in what situation, and what must they understand or do?
- What is uniquely true here, and what real proof can establish it?
- What content, data, media, and states must the surface carry?
- What must remain untouched, and what would make a polished result feel wrong?
- Which platform, performance, accessibility, localization, or delivery constraints bind the work?

Do not ask for CSS values or canned aesthetic labels.

## Brief contract

Record:

1. **Job and audience** — user, situation, need, and surface mode.
2. **Outcome and proof** — primary action or task, success, evidence, and unique mechanism.
3. **Scope and boundaries** — route, fidelity, breadth, interactivity, and anti-goals.
4. **States and ranges** — realistic minimum, typical, and maximum content plus loading, empty, error, success, overflow, and expert states.
5. **Interaction and layout** — reading/task path, hierarchy, topology, responsive behavior, and familiar affordances.
6. **Constraints** — platform, performance, accessibility, localization, source rights, and decisions the builder must not invent.

When visual direction remains open, hand the confirmed brief to `create`. Shape does not write implementation code.
