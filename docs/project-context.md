# Project Context

A cinematic template becomes a design system only when the next project can choose a different world. Write the product and design context before changing colors or adding motion.

The two short files below are enough for most projects.

## `PRODUCT.md`

```md
# Product

## Surface
<!-- landing page, dashboard, documentation, gallery, deck, etc. -->

## Users
<!-- Who is here? What do they already understand? -->

## Purpose
<!-- What must this surface help them decide, do, understand, or experience? -->

## Primary action
<!-- One action, written as a verb. -->

## Proof
<!-- Real assets, demonstrations, data, testimonials, or product behavior. -->

## Voice
<!-- Three adjectives and one sentence describing the tone. -->

## Anti-references
<!-- What would make a polished result feel generic, wrong, or off-brand? -->

## Constraints
<!-- Stack, performance, accessibility, content, legal, and launch constraints. -->
```

## `DESIGN.md`

```md
# Design

## Mode
<!-- Persuade, Operate, Read, or Experience. -->

## Creative north star
<!-- One concrete world, artifact, place, publication, or ritual. -->

## Physical scene
<!-- Who uses this, where, and under what ambient light? -->

## Color strategy
<!-- Restrained, committed, full palette, or drenched. Name the roles. -->

## Type
<!-- Display character, body character, mono usage, scale, and measure. -->

## Composition
<!-- First viewport, reading order, density, alignment, and negative space. -->

## Material
<!-- Matte, paper, lacquer, glass, metal, fabric, screen, etc. Explain why. -->

## Signature interaction
<!-- One motion or interaction that belongs to this product. -->

## Components
<!-- Existing primitives to reuse and intentional exceptions. -->

## Quality bar
<!-- Contrast, mobile, focus, reduced motion, real-copy, and performance expectations. -->
```

## Choose the surface mode

| Mode | Visitor success | Design priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Persuade | Decides and acts | Offer, proof, desire, visible action |
| Operate | Completes a task | Scanability, state, familiar controls |
| Read | Understands material | Structure, measure, rhythm, wayfinding |
| Experience | Enters the work | Artifact first, interface recedes |

A product can use several modes across different routes. Record the mode per surface rather than forcing one composition everywhere.

## Commit to a visual world

Avoid aesthetic menus made only from adjectives such as “modern,” “premium,” or “minimal.” Choose a concrete source with its own graphic grammar: an instrument panel, a field guide, a shipping manifest, a fashion lookbook, a museum catalog, a rehearsal room, a scientific plate, a transit map, or something equally specific to the audience.

The source controls more than color. Carry its typography, density, rules, materials, navigation, state language, and responsive behavior into the page.
